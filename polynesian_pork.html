Polynesian Glazed Pork Belly

Ingredients

  • 2 to 2.5 lb pork belly, cut into roughly 1-inch cubes
  • 3/4 cup pineapple juice
  • 1/2 cup soy sauce
  • 1/3 cup brown sugar
  • 4 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh ginger, grated
  • 1 tbsp rice vinegar
  • 1/2 cup pineapple chunks, optional
  • 2 green onions, sliced
  • Sesame seeds, optional

Brown the pork belly

Brown and render the pork belly

You generally do not need added oil. Pork belly will render plenty of its own fat.

  • Heat a large Dutch oven, pan over medium heat.
  • Add the pork belly in a single layer. Cook for about 10-15 minutes, turning periodically, until several sides are well browned and a significant amount of fat has rendered. You aren’t trying to cook it through yet.
  • Remove the pork belly to a plate.
  • Pour off nearly all the rendered fat, leaving about 1 tablespoon behind.

Build the sauce

  • Reduce the heat to medium-low.
  • Add the garlic and ginger to the remaining fat and cook for about 30 seconds, just until fragrant.
  • Immediately add pineapple juice, soy sauce, brown sugar
  • Stir well and scrape up the browned bits from the bottom.
  • Bring the mixture to a gentle simmer.

Reduce the Sauce with the Pork

  • Put the browned pork belly back into the pan and toss it through the sauce.
  • Reduce the heat to low.
  • Partially cover and gently simmer for approximately 45–60 minutes, turning the pieces every 10–15 minutes.
  • The goal is for the pork belly to become tender while the sauce slowly concentrates.
  • If the sauce becomes too thick before the pork is tender, add 1/4 cup water.

Reduce into a glaze

  • Once the pork is tender, remove it from the pan again.
  • Turn the heat up to medium and reduce the remaining liquid for about 5–10 minutes, stirring frequently. You want something closer to maple syrup than broth.
  • This approach is preferable to dumping cornstarch into the braise because reduction gives you a much richer, more caramelized glaze.

Glaze and caramelize

  • Return the pork belly to the reduced sauce.
  • Add the 1 tablespoon rice vinegar and optional pineapple chunks.
  • Toss over medium heat for another 2- 4 minutes, until the pork is thoroughly coated and glossy.
  • For especially caramelized edges, spread the pieces out and let individual sides sit against the hot surface for 30–60 seconds before tossing again.

Finish

  • Serve over rice
  • Top with sliced green onions and sesame seeds.
  • Taste before adding any salt—the soy sauce will generally provide plenty.
